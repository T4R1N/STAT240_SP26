Problem 6

A 95% confidence interval for the true mean monthly rent in USD in Madison based on a random sample of 100 apartments is (943, 1024). Briefly explain whether each of the following statements are true or false.

  1. About 95 percent of all rental prices in Madison are between 943 and 1024.

False: this metric refers to the mean of the population, not individual entries. There could be zero entries within that range.

  1. About 95 percent of the 100 rental prices in this sample are between 943 and 1024.

False: for the same reason as (a)

  1. In repeated sampling, the method produces intervals that include the population mean in approximately 95% of samples.

True: When we say we are 95% confident that a mean will be within that range, we can expect that the mean would be within that range 95% of the time.

  1. There is a 95% chance that the true mean monthly rent is between 943 and 1024.

False: the parameter mean is what it is, regardless of the interval we calculate. It’s not a matter of probability.

  1. Working with this same sample, we would expect a hypothesis test with significance level 0.05 to fail to reject a null hypothesis that the true mean monthly rent is 1000.

True: the null hypothesis is within our interval, meaning it’s a plausible value for the population mean.

Problem 7

For each of the three prompts, explain which condition will result in a narrower confidence interval. If the confidence level would not change, explain why.

  • Having a larger confidence level (smaller alpha) versus having a smaller confidence level (larger alpha)

Having a smaller alpha means we have a larger range of values, because more values are probable based on statistical significance. This increases our chance of having a type 1 error. A larger alpha means we have a higher threshold, narrowing the interval.

  • Having larger estimation error (a.k.a “sampling error”) versus having smaller estimation error

When the estimation error is smaller, it indicates the sample means are closer to the true population mean, leading to a narrower confidence interval.

  • Having a larger point estimate versus having a smaller point estimate

Point estimate is what the interval is constructed around; they are separate though they build upon each other.
